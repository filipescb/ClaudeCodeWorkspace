# Competitive Matrix — Engage v2

Scope: apps built around spending insights, budgeting, and financial habit-building after account connection. Excludes primarily-banking, investing, or credit-monitoring products.

## Competitors

### YNAB (You Need A Budget)

- **Core features**: Zero-based budgeting ("give every dollar a job"), envelope-style category budgets, goal tracking, shared budgets (up to 6 people per subscription).
- **Pricing**: $14.99/mo or $109/yr (34-day free trial); one tier, no feature paywalls. Discounted student program ($4.99/mo or free for a year via .edu email).
- **Target customer**: Committed, hands-on budgeters willing to actively manage a budget, not just observe spending.
- **Post-connection engagement**: Requires users to actively categorize and assign every dollar — engagement is built into the methodology itself, not bolted on via notifications.
- **Notable recent changes**: No major 2026 feature changes surfaced; product has stayed stable on its core methodology rather than expanding scope.

### Monarch Money

- **Core features**: Account sync, budgeting, net worth tracking, investment tracking, credit score tracking, receipt scanning, AI Assistant, "Household" shared access for couples.
- **Pricing**: Two-tier as of 2026 — Core at $99.99/yr (~$8.33/mo) and Plus at $199/yr (advanced forecasting, scenario modeling, priority support). 7-day free trial, full-featured (no tier gating during trial).
- **Target customer**: Couples/households managing finances together; users who want a single dashboard across budgeting, net worth, and investments.
- **Post-connection engagement**: AI Assistant and shared household view designed to keep both partners checking in; broader financial picture (not just spending) gives more reasons to open the app.
- **Notable recent changes**: Introduced the Plus tier in 2026 specifically to capture power users wanting deeper forecasting/scenario modeling — a bet that depth, not simplicity, drives retention for its most engaged segment.

### Rocket Money (formerly Truebill)

- **Core features**: Subscription tracking/cancellation, bill negotiation (paid add-on), budgeting, spending/balance alerts, Autopilot Savings (automatic transfers), spending insights.
- **Pricing**: Free tier (account linking, subscription tracking, alerts, 2 custom budget categories) + Premium, "pay what you think is fair" ($7–$14/mo). Bill negotiation charges 35–60% of first-year savings, contingent on success.
- **Target customer**: Users primarily motivated by cutting recurring costs (subscriptions, bills) rather than full budgeting discipline.
- **Post-connection engagement**: Ongoing subscription/bill monitoring creates recurring, concrete reasons to return (a canceled subscription, a negotiated bill) — engagement tied to tangible savings events, not just insight delivery.
- **Notable recent changes**: None specific surfaced; core loop (savings-through-subscription-management) has remained the primary differentiator.

### Copilot Money

- **Core features**: AI-powered auto-categorization, spending trend dashboard, investment tracking, itemized Amazon/Venmo transaction detail, budget rollover/rebalancing.
- **Pricing**: $13/mo or $95/yr (~$7.92/mo); 1-month free trial plus a no-account-required demo mode.
- **Target customer**: Design-conscious, Apple-ecosystem users (iPhone/iPad/Mac) who want a polished, native-feeling experience.
- **Post-connection engagement**: Native widgets, Apple Watch support, and Siri shortcuts keep the app ambient in the user's daily device use rather than requiring a deliberate app-open.
- **Notable recent changes**: Added web app access in December 2025 — its first step beyond an Apple-only product, though still no native Android app as of mid-2026.

### Cleo

- **Core features**: Conversational AI chatbot for spending Q&A, personalized tips and spending alerts, automatic subscription cancellation, automated bill negotiation, optional cash advances and credit-builder card.
- **Pricing**: Free tier + three paid tiers — Plus $5.99/mo (cash advances up to $250), Pro $8.99/mo (adds 2.75% APY savings + advanced AI), Builder $14.99/mo (credit-builder card, early paycheck access).
- **Target customer**: First-time budgeters and younger users who find spreadsheet-style budgeting off-putting; wants finance to feel conversational, not like homework.
- **Post-connection engagement**: Chat-based interaction model invites frequent, low-friction check-ins (asking questions) rather than passive dashboard viewing; monetary hooks (cash advance, savings APY) create recurring reasons to return tied to the user's cash flow.
- **Notable recent changes**: None specific surfaced beyond the current tiered pricing structure; positioning continues to lean into AI/chat as the primary interaction model, distinct from dashboard-first competitors.

## White Space: 2 Gaps Nudge Engage v2 Could Own

1. **Nobody combines deep personalization with a low-effort, passive experience.** YNAB and Rocket Money re-engage users through required manual work (budgeting) or app-side automation tied to a narrow use case (subscriptions/bills). Monarch and Cleo get closer to personalization but require either a household commitment (Monarch) or an active chat interaction (Cleo). None of them deliver a genuinely personalized, non-obvious insight to a user who does nothing but glance at a screen — which is exactly what Nudge's own interview and NPS research shows users want ("I need it to tell me what to do," "surface things I do not already know," "I forget it exists"). This is the core opening for the weekly summary concept.

2. **Nobody treats goal continuity as a first-class engagement mechanism.** Every competitor above treats savings/financial goals as a static setup step, not something the product actively narrates progress against over time. Rocket Money's Autopilot Savings and Monarch's Plus forecasting come closest, but neither surfaces goal progress as a recurring, front-and-center touchpoint. Given Nudge's own data (users who set a week-1 goal churn at half the rate of those who don't) and NPS feedback (a user whose goal was "never mentioned again"), consistently narrating goal progress is a differentiator none of these competitors are actively claiming.
