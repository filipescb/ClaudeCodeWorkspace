# User Interview Synthesis — Engage v2

Source: 3 interviews — Priya S. (power user, 18 months), Tom R. (churned, left after 5 weeks), Amara L. (new user, 10 days in).

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. The initial spending breakdown is a genuine "aha" moment — but a one-time hit

- Amara: *"The spending breakdown was shocking in a useful way — I had no idea I was spending that much on subscriptions."*
- Tom: *"I really wanted it to work. I connected three accounts. Set up a budget."*

### 2. After setup, the app goes static — no next step

- Tom: *"The app just showed me the same dashboard every time. I kept waiting for it to give me something to act on and it never really did."*
- Amara: *"I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day."*

### 3. Real personalized value (pattern recognition) is achievable — but takes ~3 months

- Priya: *"It started surfacing patterns I had not consciously registered — like that I always overspend in the last week of the month."*
- Priya: *"It took about three months to get there and I think most people give up before that."*

### 4. Users want the app to actively prompt them, not just display data

- Tom: *"I switched to YNAB because at least that feels like it is asking something of me."*
- Amara: *"I have been waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step."*

### 5. Sustained relevance turns usage into ritual

- Priya: *"I open Nudge every Sunday morning before I start my week. It has become a ritual."*
- Priya: *"I feel like Nudge actually knows my financial habits now."*

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Time-to-value vs. patience users actually have**: Priya's payoff (the app "knows" her) took ~3 months to arrive organically — but Tom churned at 5 weeks and Amara is already disengaging at 10 days. The value Priya describes is real, but the current experience doesn't survive long enough for most users to reach it.
- **The aha moment is compelling but insufficient**: Both Amara and Tom describe the initial spending breakdown positively, undercutting any assumption that the problem is the first-touch experience — the drop-off happens *after* a successful first impression, not because of a weak one.
- **Active prompting vs. passive insight-checking**: Tom and Amara both want the app to proactively tell them what to do next. Priya's ritual, by contrast, is self-directed — she goes looking for patterns on her own schedule. This suggests two different engagement models may be needed depending on how far along a user is, not a single one-size-fits-all fix.

## Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

New and soon-to-churn users are stranded in the gap between the first aha moment (the spending breakdown) and the second one (personalized pattern recognition) — a gap that currently takes Nudge about three months to close organically, far longer than users are willing to wait. Nothing in the current experience closes that gap early. This directly supports the hypothesis behind the personalized weekly summary concept: the goal isn't to repeat the first insight, it's to manufacture an early version of Priya's "it knows me" moment well before month three, and to pair it with an explicit next action — not just another dashboard view — so users like Tom and Amara have something to act on instead of waiting.
